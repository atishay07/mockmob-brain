The owner (17 Oct 2026, about 11:15 pm): results were weaker and usage higher partly because Opus subagents were spawned to do the thinking. "The orchestrated thinker does all the heavy lifting, and agents are just the doers, and they report back." Every handover between agents loses some context, so leaving design taste and decisions to subagents costs quality and speed. This is not about cost, and he does not want a rule of "fewer Opus agents". It is about allotting tasks clearly; the right mix of mostly Sonnet and some Opus follows from that.

**Why:** briefs like "decide and build the better version" or "write a decision note and build it" handed the judgment to agents. They interpreted his taste differently each time, so round after round missed his intent; the rejected result/review redesign is one example.

**How to apply:**
- Before spawning, the main session decides: the exact layout, hierarchy, copy, states, motion values and acceptance checks, in his words where possible.
- The brief is a spec to implement and prove. It is not a question to answer.
- Sonnet implements specs.
- Opus only where the doing itself needs taste the spec cannot fully carry: fine motion polish, a visual critique against his references, a hard design trade-off the main session explicitly delegates.
- Read agent outputs critically and own the decisions.
