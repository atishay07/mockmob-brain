On 16 Nov 2026 (about 5:45 pm IST) the owner looked at www.mockmob.in on his phone after a day of building and wrote: "looks good on the desktop page but falls short on the mobile page", "way below expectation", "this won't cut it". His words are whole in mockmob-brain/docs/OWNER-MASTER-LEDGER.md (LK266-LK275). The specifics he gave:

- A feature he calls "not very important" (Daily Five, Sunday Mock) must not sit under the hero and must not take a screen of height. Two full calendar pages (about 1,500 px on a phone) were the fault, though he had approved that look the night before.
- A label nobody can decode is a fault ("Number 2 ... What exactly does it mean?"). Say what a thing is in one sentence, including which subjects.
- A block whose words do not share one left edge, or that stacks three cards on a phone, reads to him as "broken ... placement is very weird".
- Things that move without being asked on a phone read as "weird movements": the held scroll swapping screens, the companion Mobi leaning and hopping, a score sweeping by itself, a slider set by a scrolling thumb.
- "Not just walls of text, and that is also not properly lined": a how-it-works page of six headings over six paragraphs failed. Figures and a timeline are the form.
- No pulsing or flashing dots, no slop fonts.
- When a block promises a capability he asks whether the product really has it and what it costs ("uploading pictures ... how many credits ... pricing it properly"). A landing claim must be backed by the feature and its price.
- "The codebase is yours. Feel free to make any changes that you like, so don't be hesitant on that part or keep asking me stuff."

**Why:** He judges on his phone only; an earlier yes to a look does not survive seeing it take too much room live. He was disappointed after a long build day, so the cost of getting the phone wrong is high.

**How to apply:** Before shipping any landing section, walk it at 390 px with real finger swipes and ask for each section: how important is this to him (that sets its place and its height), can a 16-year-old say what it is from one sentence, do all words start on one edge, does anything move that the reader did not start, is any explanation a wall of text. Size and place by importance, not by how much was built. Decide and ship; do not ask him about reversible design choices. My interpretation (not his words): a section's height on the phone should be earned by its importance to conversion.
